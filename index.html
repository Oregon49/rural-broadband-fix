3. When will I get fiber?

Honest answer: there's no public date for your address. Here's what IS known about Oregon's plan:

  • Right now (Oct 2026): all provider contracts are signed. Most construction starts late 2026.
  • Federal deadline: providers have 4 years from signing — so about September 2030.
  • Not everyone gets fiber. Oregon's approved plan: 52.5% fiber, 46.4% satellite, 1.1% fixed wireless. Nearly half of funded locations are getting satellite, not fiber.
Why no exact date? Each provider's build schedule lives inside its contract with the state, and those schedules aren't published. The state runs an address lookup showing which technology and company will serve you — but not when.

If your address shows "LEO satellite," that's Starlink or Amazon — you're not waiting on fiber at all.

8. What a year offline costs

Waiting has a price tag too — paid by the household, not the program:

  • $1,950/year per household — the average economic value of broadband access. Across 6.3 million unconnected rural co-op households, that's $68.2 billion in lost value (National Rural Electric Cooperative Association).
  • $370,000/year per rural hospital — the cost of not having telemedicine, in a study of 24 rural hospitals (USDA Economic Research Service).
  • Higher incomes where broadband lands — a peer-reviewed study by an Oklahoma State economist found rural broadband adoption associated with higher median household income and more small businesses.

Every year a family waits for a trench is a year of remote work, telehealth, and online schooling they don't get.

9. The honest limitations

Starlink is not fiber. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. Here's what the data says:

  • Rain. Starlink uses Ku/Ka-band radio, which rain absorbs. A Finnish university study measured 38% slower downloads and 52% slower uploads during rain (service stayed up 98.5%+ of the time). In rainy western Oregon, expect slowdowns in storms — not outages, but slower.
  • Latency. Starlink runs 20–60 ms vs. fiber's 5–20 ms. Fine for video calls and streaming; competitive gamers will feel the difference.
  • Uploads. Starlink: 10–20 Mbps up. Fiber: often symmetrical gigabit. Video creators and heavy uploaders notice.
  • Lifespan. A dish lasts roughly 5–7 years; fiber in the ground lasts 30–50. Over decades, fiber wins on durability — that's the genuine tradeoff.
  • Congestion. Speeds dip at peak evening hours as more users share a satellite cell. Fiber doesn't have this problem.

We show this because the argument for Starlink doesn't need hype — it needs the math to hold up under scrutiny. It does.
